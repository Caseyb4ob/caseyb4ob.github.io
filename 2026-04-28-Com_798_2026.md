## Intro

## Recruiting Video
For this assignment, we were asked to make a video targeted at high school students explaining what we do and why it might be interesting to them. Part of the goal of the assignment was to practice explaining our work to a high school‑educated audience. I found it useful for this reason, but definitely different from similar exercises I have done with actual students in front of me. When I talk directly to a person, I can tailor what I say to them, but creating this type of general verbal content was new to me.

## Research Summary
Here, we were asked to provide a summary of a recent paper in our field. The target audience was non‑experts, which I interpreted as a science‑interested group that may not have specific knowledge of my field. Additionally, we were asked to have AI create a second version of the assignment. This last part was interesting because it made me realise that I could do a much better job of breaking up my text for this type of writing to increase readability.

## Commentary/ OpEd
This assignment prompted us to write an Op‑Ed requesting a specific call to action for a target audience. My target audience was that of ‘The Conversation’, a popular science outlet. That way, the target would be people who are at least science‑interested. This was probably the hardest assignment for me because I had trouble finding a good call to action. I ended up choosing that a push for a gene drive field test was the best option. I do believe a field test would be good, but I am not confident my arguments were actually compelling in this piece.

## Policy One-Pager
Here we were meant to create a “one‑pager” that we would leave behind with a politician. The goal was for the one‑pager to have enough information to gain interest but not overwhelm, and also to give the reader contact information to follow up. I thought this was a useful, interesting assignment, similar to an activity I had done as part of GGS years ago for a different project.

## Mini-TED Talk
While I do not have access to the video for this, I gave a “TED‑style” short talk on gene drives. A large part of the difficulty for this talk was not having access to any visual aids. Generally, when I give a talk on my research to any audience, visuals are very useful. They help both to keep me grounded and on track and give the audience another way of understanding what I am saying. Completing this activity without visual aids was useful and difficult.

## STEM Podcast
This was one of the more fun assignments; we were asked to make a podcast with some of our classmates. I was put in a fun group with an entomologist and a social scientist. The three of us come from very distinct backgrounds, and I think that helped make the podcast more interesting.

My biggest challenge in this podcast was editing the podcast down to the 7‑minute mark. I spent five hours in the library trying to do this. After cutting much of the content, I spent a lot of time cutting long silences and “ums”. I also had to tie the pieces around the cut content together, which was harder than expected. In the end, I only got it down to 7:40, but luckily, my group mate was able to finish it off for me.
